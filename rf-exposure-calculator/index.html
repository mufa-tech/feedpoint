How the distance is worked out

The FCC lets amateurs evaluate exposure with the far-field equations of OET Bulletin 65. Power density at distance R, with the EPA ground reflection factor (field × 1.6, power × 2.56), is

S = 2.56 × EIRP / (4π R²)

Set S to the limit and solve for R. EIRP is the time-averaged power at the antenna times the antenna gain: PEP × mode factor × share of time on the air, less the feedline loss. The result is shown with ground reflection, and without it for comparison.

Controlled and uncontrolled limits

47 CFR §1.1310 sets two tiers. Controlled (occupational) applies to you and to people who know about the exposure and can do something about it, averaged over 6 minutes. Uncontrolled (general population) applies to neighbours, family and passers-by, averaged over 30 minutes. Below 1.34 MHz and above 30 MHz the limits are flat; between, they fall with the square of the frequency, which is why 10 m needs more distance than 40 m at the same power.

Mode factors and time on the air

Time on the air is the busiest part of the averaging window. With an on/off cycle the calculator takes the window that starts with a key-down: 5 minutes on and 5 off fills a whole 6-minute window (100 %), but only 15 of 30 minutes.

The exemption test

The FCC rules of 2020 added §1.1307(b)(3)(i)(C): it exempts a station whose ERP at a given distance is at or below a threshold, for example 3450 R² / f² watts between 1.34 and 30 MHz. Enter the distance to people and the calculator runs the test with your 30-minute average ERP. The table is only valid at least λ/2π from the antenna; closer than that, the full evaluation above applies.

Close to the antenna

Inside λ/2π the far-field equations do not strictly hold. OET-65 notes that for wire antennas they over-predict there, which errs on the safe side, so the result still stands with a note. Small transmitting loops are the exception: their near field is stronger than the estimate, so tick Magnetic loop and the calculator gives no distance inside λ/2π.

Outside the United States

This page uses the US rules. The app also evaluates by the German BNetzA method with the 26. BImSchV limits, by the ICNIRP 2020 general public levels that Ofcom requires in the UK, and by ICNIRP 2020 or 1998 for other countries, and saves each evaluation as a dated record.
